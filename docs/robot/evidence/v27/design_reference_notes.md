# v27 cosmetic casing reference review

Reviewed 2026-09-30. Scope: original, removable printed covers around the unchanged v26 skeleton. This is visual direction for the native CAD author, not an actuator redesign or a clearance/strength certificate. All ten downloaded images were opened and inspected. Exact source URLs, local paths, pixel sizes, byte counts and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in [visual_reference_manifest.json](references/visual_reference_manifest.json). The official imagery is retained locally as reference material, not as artwork for the robot.

## Existing robot and the strongest direction

Baseline inspected: [v26 whole robot](../v26/v26_whole_robot_final.png). Its rounded-square light head, dark camera bezel and broad pale torso already establish a clear visual identity. The open arm forks, exposed thigh motors and thin lower-leg rails make the limbs look less finished and much lighter than the torso. The waist/hip area has many overlapping circular parts. Pale hands and dark shoulder housings already fit a pale-shell/dark-joint palette.

The recommended change is continuous **tapered limb volume**, with **deliberate dark breaks at moving joints**. Preserve the existing head and torso proportions. Use broad smooth surfaces, a small number of purposeful panel seams and limited accents. The most useful references are R1/G1 for limb segmentation, Apollo for rounded plate outlines, and current Atlas for leaving the waist mechanisms visibly separate from the torso.

## Guidance for the native cover pass

| Region | Proposed form | Constraints on this skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| Shins/calves | One long softly convex front surface; a broader rear calf in its upper half; gradual distal taper; soft central front ridge rather than a sharp armor point. Side/rear split seam follows the rail envelope. | Leave motor/encoder service windows and ankle differential/link paths open. A knee-adjacent cover end must not sweep into the thigh. Follow actual current axes, not an assumed human knee direction. |
| Thighs | Rounded front/outer covers around the existing volume, fullest near the upper third and narrower toward the knee. End above the moving knee assembly. | Inner faces need more cutback than outer faces. Do not bridge hip yaw/roll/pitch components with one rigid printed shell or cover sprockets with an unverified closed envelope. |
| Hip/pelvis/waist | Two compact lateral hip wings and small softened local faces, with a narrow dark waist band. Use rounded lateral transitions instead of adding a large solid central block. | Hand-to-hip rest clearance was about 19 mm per side in v26 at 6° abduction and neutral hips; this is not a full sweep result. New outward hip volume directly consumes that space. Each wing must belong to one rigid link. Preserve waist and leg swing openings. |
| Torso | Keep the existing large shell; improve transitions and use one modest recessed service-panel outline or a few shallow real vent slots. Continue the head's rounded rectangular language. | Details should correspond to service/airflow needs. Retain battery/electronics access and the verified neck opening. Do not add dense arbitrary surface grooves or narrow nonprinting fins. |
| Upper arms | Smooth front/back volume fitted around each open fork, with rounded ends and gradual taper toward the elbow. Keep the dark shoulder disc readable. | Cover and fasteners move with the upper-arm fork only. Leave driven flange/rear bearing and elbow screw access. Avoid making the shoulder cylinder appear much wider through a thick decorative cap. |
| Forearms | Slightly fuller upper forearm transitioning to a narrower wrist, with a long smooth outer plane and side seam. A single subtle longitudinal feature can echo the shin fronts. | Preserve the current asymmetric left/right wrist adapter patterns and M3 access. AmazingHand palm clearance, cable entry and wrist bolts take precedence over a closed sleeve. Do not mirror left commercial hand geometry blindly. |

These forms are original recommendations, not measured profiles copied from reference robots. Set dimensions from the v26 bodies and moving envelopes. Dark visual shadow gaps can look intentional, but their appearance alone does not prove moving clearance. The root's native cover check must establish actual fit, joint-opening margins and unaffected mechanism placements.

## Consistent finish and printable organization

- Primary shell color: neutral pale gray/off-white close to the existing torso/head/hands.
- Secondary: graphite joint caps, open waist bands, real vent recesses and shadow gaps. Keep purchased motors/bearings visually distinct rather than repainting them as plastic.
- Accent: one restrained blue/teal family on a small number of repeated panels; do not distribute unrelated orange/red/blue markings across every segment. Existing orange transmission hardware can remain a material-coded mechanism detail.
- Repeat the same broad-radius shell ends, modest longitudinal taper and seam widths across arms and legs. Avoid near-perfect cylindrical sleeves at every limb and avoid arbitrary armor spikes.
- Use separately named left/right cosmetics grouped under their rigid parent links. Prototype front/rear or inner/outer split shells with accessible screws; final attachment inserts, wall thickness and print orientation require their own fit and load review. No cover should tie together two independently moving components.
- A starting visual wall of roughly 2.5–3 mm is a provisional CAD choice, not a strength or heat recommendation. Keep hot motor/driver air paths and wiring bend space open. Prefer shallow recessed service details over unnecessary heavy embossed blocks.
